# Website References & UI/UX Features for Lee AnCheng

This document outlines standout website references, their key UI/UX features, and proposals for how they can be adapted for the modern brush and landscape ink artist Lee AnCheng.

## 1. Rijksmuseum (Rijksstudio)
* **URL:** [https://www.rijksmuseum.nl/en/rijksstudio](https://www.rijksmuseum.nl/en/rijksstudio)
* **Standout UX Feature:** "Deep Zoom" capability allowing users to pinch and zoom into gigapixel scans of classic artworks to explore microscopic details.
* **Proposal for Lee AnCheng:** Ink wash relies heavily on the organic interaction between water, ink, and Xuan paper. Implement an interactive, ultra-high-resolution image viewer so visitors can inspect the bleeding edges, brush pressure, and *qiyun* (rhythmic vitality) of his strokes up close without losing image fidelity.

## 2. David Zwirner (Platform Viewing Rooms)
* **URL:** [https://www.davidzwirner.com/](https://www.davidzwirner.com/)
* **Standout UX Feature:** "Studio" viewing rooms that combine clean, white-cube digital galleries with embedded audio clips, archival photos, and curator notes integrated seamlessly into a vertical scroll.
* **Proposal for Lee AnCheng:** Instead of a basic image grid, structure his eras (e.g., the airy Keelung Period vs. the heavier Kaohsiung Period) as narrative scrolling viewing rooms. Intersperse his paintings with photos of the Taiwanese landscapes that inspired him and archival text explaining his solitary process.

## 3. James Turrell (Official Site)
* **URL:** [https://jamesturrell.com/](https://jamesturrell.com/)
* **Standout UX Feature:** Atmospheric UI. The website uses subtle, slow-fading background color transitions tied to the user's scroll depth, mimicking the artist's physical light installations.
* **Proposal for Lee AnCheng:** Lee's late works balance dense black ink with a "penetrating sense of light". Tie the website's background theme to the scroll, slowly shifting from bright, airy tones (representing his early fluid style) to deeper, high-contrast dark modes as the user scrolls into his heavier, post-2000s works.

## 4. TeamLab (Digital Art Collective)
* **URL:** [https://www.teamlab.art/](https://www.teamlab.art/)
* **Standout UX Feature:** Immersive, full-viewport video backgrounds and WebGL fluid interactions that react to the user's cursor, making the interface feel alive and boundaryless.
* **Proposal for Lee AnCheng:** Use a subtle WebGL fluid distortion effect for page transitions or image hovers. When a user moves their mouse over a thumbnail, the image could ripple or reveal an ink-bleed transition, echoing his experimental blending and rendering methods.

## 5. Studio Olafur Eliasson
* **URL:** [https://olafureliasson.net/](https://olafureliasson.net/)
* **Standout UX Feature:** A highly advanced, multi-axis filtering system (the "Archive"). Users can organically sort artworks by color, medium, year, or concept using an interactive, fluid grid.
* **Proposal for Lee AnCheng:** Because Lee frequently shifted between figurative and abstract styles throughout his life, an interactive timeline slider combined with a dynamic grid would allow collectors to visually track the evolution of his ink weight and abstraction over time.

## 6. Horizontal-Scroll Portfolios (Awwwards WebGL Standards)
* **URL:** [https://www.awwwards.com/websites/horizontal-scrolling/](https://www.awwwards.com/websites/horizontal-scrolling/)
* **Standout UX Feature:** Horizontal, gesture-driven galleries. Instead of scrolling down, the user's mouse wheel or trackpad swipe pulls them sideways through a curated, floating exhibition space.
* **Proposal for Lee AnCheng:** Traditional Asian ink art is often formatted as horizontal handscrolls. A horizontal-scrolling UI would physically mirror the historical method of "unrolling" and viewing ink landscapes, modernizing an ancient format through smooth web interactions.
